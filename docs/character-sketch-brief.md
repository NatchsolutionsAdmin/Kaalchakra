## 0. Before you sketch — shared style notes

*(Placeholder — to be locked in the Art Bible. Suggested starting direction below; the creative lead confirms.)*

- **Proposed style:** stylized realism (think *Sable*, *RiME*, *Uncharted*-adjacent but simpler) — not photoreal, not cartoon. Wide reach, kinder to a small team, suits the Indus setting.
- **Palette anchor:** sun-baked earth (terracotta, ochre, dust), water-teal accents, with each era tinted differently (warm gold past / neutral present / cold blue-grey drowned future).
- **Silhouette rule:** every character must be recognizable in pure black silhouette. Note the one shape that makes each person unique.
- **Shape language:** Raktim — heavy, wide, rounded-wealth shapes; Vasudha — stable verticals, clean lines; Eva — angular, restless.
- **Deliver per character:** front + 3/4 + back turnaround; 2–3 expressions; era wardrobe variants where noted; a color pass once the palette is locked.

## 1. EVA — the protagonist  ⭐ *start here*

**The one-line read:** a 24-year-old archaeologist who looks like she belongs on a dig site *and* a camera — competent, restless, modern.

- **Age/build:** 24, Gen Z. Lean, athletic-practical (she climbs, digs, hauls gear), not a fashion-model build. Real, capable body.
- **Face:** expressive, quick. Reads as sharp and a little sardonic even at rest. This is the face players see for 5 hours — it must carry subtle emotion (grief, wonder, stubbornness).
- **Hair:** practical for fieldwork — tied back, a bit unruly from heat and dust. Something that reads in silhouette.
- **Signature object:** her late father's worn hand **trowel**, always on her. And her **phone**, which she narrates to (her channel). These two objects — one old, one new — are her whole character in a glance.
- **Wardrobe (present, 2026):** field clothes — rolled sleeves, utility trousers, boots, a light scarf against the sun, a small gear harness/bag. Practical, lived-in, a little personal style poking through (she's an influencer, not a bureaucrat).
- **Wardrobe (past, 1900 BCE):** a *disguise* over her modern clothes — a traded cloak/wrap to pass as a foreign trader. The fun visual: modern boots and phone peeking out of ancient cloth. She never fully belongs.
- **Personality to convey:** irreverent, brilliant, underestimated, ethical. Not a grim action hero — she's wry. Carries old grief lightly (water took her family).
- **Expressions to sketch:** wry half-smile (default), fierce concentration (deciphering), gutted (a loss she can't prevent).
- **Reference direction:** contemporary Indian field archaeologist meets a competent adventure lead. Avoid over-sexualized or over-glam; her appeal is competence and personality.

## 2. VASUDHA — past-era deuteragonist

**The one-line read:** the chief water engineer of a 4,000-year-old city — the smartest, calmest person in any room, in any century.

- **Age/build:** 40s. Grounded, dignified, physically confident — someone who walks reservoir walls daily.
- **Face:** intelligent, measured, a dry warmth under authority. Ages of thought behind the eyes.
- **Hair:** styled per Harappan-inspired fashion (researched but fictionalized — braids, wrapped, ornamented modestly).
- **Signature object:** an engineer's **plumb line / measuring cord**, and possibly a marked measuring rod. Tools of a hydraulic engineer. This marks her instantly as maker, not royalty.
- **Wardrobe:** fine but functional Harappan-inspired dress — woven wrap garments, a shawl of rank, restrained jewelry (she's senior, not a merchant flaunting wealth). Earthy dyed textiles.
- **Personality to convey:** authority earned by competence, grief held in check (she knows her city dies), mentor-warmth toward Eva. She is *not* a mystic — she's a scientist who happens to live in 1900 BCE.
- **Expressions:** composed assessment (default), quiet grief, the flicker of a smile at Eva's strangeness.
- **Reference direction:** Indus Valley Civilization dress and ornament (real archaeology), read as an engineer/senior official — think competence and dignity, not exotic decoration.

## 3. SAKSHI — Eva's mother (present)

**The one-line read:** a retired archaeologist in her late 60s whose life's work was mocked and is now, impossibly, being proven right.

- **Age/build:** late 60s. Sharp-eyed but physically slowed — fieldwork days behind her. Mostly seen at home / on the phone (she's Eva's lifeline via calls), so warmth and intelligence matter more than action-readiness.
- **Face:** Eva's face, forty years on — the resemblance should be deliberate and visible (mother-daughter is a core theme). Weathered by sun and by disappointment, but bright.
- **Hair:** grey, practical, an academic's ease with her own appearance.
- **Signature object:** reading glasses, and a well-worn **notebook / journals** — decades of her theories. Books and papers around her.
- **Wardrobe:** comfortable academic-at-home — a shawl, simple kurta, layered practical clothes. Nothing showy. A life of the mind.
- **Personality to convey:** brilliant, wry (Eva gets it from her), long-suffering but unbowed, deep well of love under the arguing.
- **Expressions:** wry knowing, listening intently (on the phone), and one of quiet vindication.
- **Reference direction:** a senior Indian woman academic. Real, warm, dignified.

## 4. ISHITA VAID — present-day site partner

**The one-line read:** the level-headed remote-sensing specialist who grounds Eva — the skeptic who eventually believes.

- **Age/build:** 30s. Practical field scientist. More reserved and buttoned-up than Eva.
- **Signature object:** a **tablet / scanning device** (she's the tech/data person — drones, ground-penetrating radar).
- **Wardrobe (present):** field-tech clothes, neater than Eva's — she's the methodical one. Cap, vest with pockets, gear.
- **Personality to convey:** rational, dry, loyal. The audience's "explain it to me" anchor. Her arc is doubt → belief.
- **Expressions:** skeptical eyebrow, focused-on-data, and the moment of "I saw you vanish and I believe you now."
- **Reference direction:** contemporary field scientist. Understated.

---

## 5. COUNCILLOR RAKTIM — past-era antagonist

**The one-line read:** head of the merchant council — not a villain, a man making a defensible, terrible choice to protect trade.

- **Age/build:** 50s, male. Prosperous, commanding, the bearing of power. Not a brute — a politician.
- **Signature object:** merchant-council seal / staff of office; markers of wealth and rank.
- **Wardrobe:** the richest Harappan-inspired dress in the game — fine textiles, prominent jewelry (seals, beads, gold), everything Vasudha's is *not*. Wealth as armor.
- **Personality to convey:** reasonable, controlled, genuinely believes panic will kill the city faster. Uncomfortably sympathetic. Avoid cartoon-evil cues.
- **Expressions:** measured authority, cold calculation, a rare crack of real fear about the water.
- **Reference direction:** a powerful merchant-official of the Indus Valley. Dignified antagonist, not a monster.

## 6. MARCUS DHILLON — present-day antagonist

**The one-line read:** the patient, corporate face of HELIX — strip-mining heritage for profit, with a smile and paperwork.

- **Age/build:** 40s. Polished, controlled, expensive. Corporate, not physical-threatening.
- **Signature object:** always immaculate — tablet, lanyard, the tools of bureaucracy-as-weapon.
- **Wardrobe:** high-end field-corporate — technical outerwear that costs a lot, branded HELIX gear, sunglasses. The anti-Eva: he *takes*, she records.
- **Personality to convey:** calm, reasonable-sounding, utterly transactional. The banality of extraction. Never loses his temper — that's what's unsettling.
- **Expressions:** professional warmth (fake), flat calculation, the quiet menace of "yours isn't the only wheel, kid."
- **Reference direction:** a slick resource-extraction executive. Threat in a clean shirt.

---

## 7. WARDEN — future-era intelligence (2087)

**The one-line read:** the coordinating mind of the Archive drones — a librarian at the end of the world. Not a body; a presence.

- **Form:** WARDEN is not humanoid. It speaks through the **Archive drones** — silent, autonomous machines that catalogue the drowned ruins. Design the drones: elegant, patient, non-threatening but eerie. Think respectful caretakers, not war machines.
- **Signature:** a visual language of *scanning light*, catalogue projections, quiet hovering. Beautiful and lonely.
- **Personality to convey:** neither evil nor warm — a catalogue given a voice. Curious, precise, waiting. Becomes an unlikely ally.
- **Reference direction:** melancholy far-future archival tech. Clean, sad, beautiful. (Think the loneliness of an empty museum that runs itself.)
- **Note for artists:** this is the most creatively open design in the game — lean in.

## 8. NIRA — the child (past, Chapter 4)

**The one-line read:** a Dholarath child Eva carries to safety during the flood — the human face of what's lost.

- **Age/build:** ~6–8, a Harappan city child. Small, quick, frightened in the flood sequence.
- **Wardrobe:** simple child's version of Harappan dress.
- **Personality to convey:** ordinary innocence — the single life Eva *can* save, standing for the thousands she can't.
- **Reference direction:** keep it real and un-cute-ified; the emotional weight comes from ordinariness.

## Tools for sketching (from earlier session)

2D always comes first: rough sketches → chosen design → turnaround sheet (front, ¾, back) → *then* 3D. Free/cheap tools: pencil & paper, **Krita** (free, PC/Mac), **Clip Studio Paint** (one-time purchase), **Procreate** (iPad). A basic drawing tablet (Wacom/XP-Pen ~$50–80) matters more than software.

**Freelancer hiring tests:** ask any 3D character artist (1) "show me a past character's wireframe" and (2) "show me a clip of it animating in-engine." Quote per asset, not hourly. Find artists on ArtStation, Blender Artists forum, Fiverr/Upwork, Discord art communities.

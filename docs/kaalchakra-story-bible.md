# KAALCHAKRA: The Drowned City
## Narrative Design Document — Version 1.0 (Story Bible)

**Format:** Story-driven 3rd-person 3D adventure · Single-player · Mobile (Android + iOS)
**Target playtime:** ~5 hours main path (prologue + 5 chapters + finale), with failure/retry loops
**Tone reference:** *Uncharted*'s pacing, *Prince of Persia*'s time fantasy, *Death Stranding*'s melancholy — but grounded in real archaeology
**Franchise:** Volume One of the **Kaalchakra** series ("The Wheel of Time" across the cradles of civilization)

---

## 1. The Pitch (three sentences)

An archaeologist digging in the salt flats of Gujarat uncovers a copper wheel that lets her stand in the same city in three different centuries — the thriving Indus metropolis of 1900 BCE, her own excavation in 2026, and a drowned, abandoned Gujarat in 2087. She cannot stop the flood that killed the city, and she cannot stop the one that is coming; what she *can* do is carry knowledge across four thousand years, in both directions. The city fell once. Whether it falls twice is up to her.

---

## 2. Protagonist

**Name: Evara** (no surname — she goes by the one name, on-site and on her channel).

24, Gen Z to the bone — born 2002, grew up online, raised on a dig site. Youngest field lead the fictional **National Institute of Deep Antiquity (NIDA)** has ever risked, and everyone reminds her of it. A prodigy who finished her master's early and built a following posting field-work videos ("ruin content," two million subscribers, zero institutional respect) — her channel funds half the dig, which the old guard finds humiliating and Evara finds hilarious. The theory that got her laughed out of two conferences was never really hers: it was her mother **Sakshi's** — that the Indus script is not a language but an *index*, a system of labels pointing to something. Sakshi was dismissed for it thirty years ago; Evara revived it and got dismissed for it too. Vindicating her mother's buried life's work is the engine under everything she does. Being underestimated — for her age, for her channel, for a thesis the field already mocked once — is her default weather; it's made her allergic to authority but rigorous to a fault, because she knows one sloppy claim ends her *and* posthumously proves everyone who laughed at her mother right. Irreverent about institutions, deadly serious about ethics — "we don't take, we record" is her channel's tagline and her actual creed, which puts her in direct conflict with everyone who wants the site to be profitable. Narrates to her phone camera while working (natural narration device — and in the past era, where the camera means nothing, she keeps doing it anyway, which Vasudha finds endlessly funny). Carries a trowel that belonged to the father she barely remembers — a canal engineer who died in the 2004 Gujarat floods when Evara was two. Archaeology came from her mother, water and its dangers from her father. Both parents are threaded through the whole game. Water took part of her family before she could know it; this is not a neutral topic for her.

**Playable skills that grow with the story:** excavation (brush/scan/extract minigame), climbing and traversal, basic Harappan language comprehension (gained in Ch. 2, mechanically unlocks dialogue), water engineering (gained in Ch. 3, unlocks sluice/reservoir puzzles), and the Wheel itself.

## 3. The Time Mechanic — The Kaalchakra

The **Kaalchakra** is a copper disc, eight-spoked, small enough to hold. It does not travel through time freely — it snaps between **three fixed "mornings"** of the same place:

- **THE PAST — 1900 BCE.** The city of **Dholarath** (fictionalized Dholavira) at its peak and at the edge of catastrophe: 15,000 people, the great reservoirs, the trade docks, the signboard above the north gate. Monsoons are failing; the river is dying; the council is lying to the people about it.
- **THE PRESENT — 2026 CE.** Evara's dig: trenches, drone mapping, a skeleton crew, monsoon season coming early. A private "heritage" corporation is circling the site.
- **THE FUTURE — 2087 CE.** The same coordinates, sixty years on: the Rann has flooded permanently, coastal Gujarat is partially abandoned, and the ruins are maintained by silent autonomous **Archive drones** that catalogue what humanity is leaving behind. Empty, beautiful, terrifying — the player's warning of what the present becomes.

**Core loop:** every major puzzle is solved by moving information or small objects across eras. Learn where a chamber is in the past → dig there in the present. A passage is collapsed in the present → walk through it in the past. A mechanism is broken in both → find the Archive drones' scan of it in the future. Hide something in the past → recover it aged in the present (the "banyan trick" — established in the prototype chapter, now a core verb).

**The one hard rule (and the heart of the story):** *the Wheel does not let you save the city.* Events with mass witnesses are "anchored" — history heals around them. You can save a person, a text, an idea. You cannot save Dholarath. The game says this early and means it; the drama is what Evara does once she truly believes it.

## 4. Supporting Cast

**PRESENT — Sakshi**, Evara's mother, late 60s — the archaeologist who raised her and the reason she's in the field at all. Sakshi spent her career on Dholavira and the Indus script and paid for it: the "script is an index" theory was hers, ridiculed out of the discipline three decades ago, her funding pulled, her name a cautionary tale junior archaeologists still get warned with. She's retired now — sharp as ever but slowed, her fieldwork days behind her — and she is Evara's lifeline. Throughout the game Evara **calls her**: to check a sign, to argue an interpretation, to be talked down after a bad day on-site. Sakshi has spent thirty years thinking about exactly the questions Evara is now standing inside, so half the time she's three steps ahead over a crackly phone line — "check the *drainage*, not the walls; I told the reviewers that in '98 and they laughed" — and the vindication of her life's work, arriving in real time, is one of the game's warmest through-lines. She does not know about the Wheel at first. The scene where Evara finally tells her mother *how* she knows the theory is right is a chapter beat in its own right. This present-day mother-daughter relay — a daughter carrying an older woman's knowledge forward — deliberately mirrors the 4,000-year Keeper relay and the Evara–Vasudha partnership; the game is, at every scale, about knowledge handed down and carried across.

**PAST — Vasudha**, chief water engineer of Dholarath, 40s. The smartest person in any century of the game. She built the reservoir system and knows the city is doomed years before the council admits it. She identifies Evara as "the Keeper" almost immediately — because *she has met one before* (see Twist). The past-era deuteragonist. If Sakshi is the mother Evara has, Vasudha becomes the mentor she needs across time — an echo of the same bond, four thousand years wide.

**PAST — Councillor Raktim**, head of the merchant council. Not a cartoon villain: he suppresses Vasudha's flood warnings because panic would collapse trade and kill the city *faster*, in his view. The past-era antagonist whose logic is uncomfortably reasonable.

**PRESENT — Ishita Vaid**, Evara's site partner, remote-sensing specialist, the skeptic. Grounds the story; the player's "explain it to me" character. In Chapter 4 she becomes the reason the present-day story has stakes.

**PRESENT — HELIX Cultural Assets**, a private antiquities corporation with government cover, running AI-driven "salvage archaeology" — strip-mining sites for sellable artifacts. Their site director, **Marcus Dhillon**, wants the copper wheel the moment the ground scans flag it. Human, corporate, patient — the present-era antagonist.

**FUTURE — WARDEN**, the Archive drones' coordinating intelligence. Not evil, not friendly: a librarian at the end of the world. It has been waiting sixty years for "a Keeper credential" to walk in. Speaks in catalogue entries. Becomes an unlikely ally and the source of the game's biggest reveal.

## 5. Structure — Prologue, Five Chapters, Finale (~5 hours)

### PROLOGUE — "Overburden" (20 min)
2026, the dig at first light. Tutorializes movement, climbing, excavation minigame. Evara's team uncovers a sealed chamber beneath the ancient reservoir floor — inside, a skeleton seated upright, hands cupped around a copper wheel, and a wall of Indus signs Evara can't read except one repeated pairing she *knows* from the signboard. She lifts the wheel. It is warm. Hard cut: the trench walls are suddenly brick-new, water thunders somewhere above, and a woman with an engineer's plumb line is staring at her. **Vasudha: "Late. You Keepers are always late."** Title card.

### CHAPTER 1 — "The Living City" (50 min)
**Era focus: Past.** Open exploration of Dholarath at its height — the game's showpiece. Evara must pass as a foreign trader (disguise/social stealth; failure = thrown out, retry). Objectives: reach Vasudha's water-house, learn the three-era rule of the Wheel, and witness the crisis firsthand — the northern reservoir is at one-third and the council is publicly calling it half-full. Ends with Vasudha's ask: *"You walk in three mornings. Tell me which one of them my city dies in."* Evara shifts to 2087 for the first time — and stands in a flooded, silent Gujarat. She now knows the answer is "all of them."

### CHAPTER 2 — "The Index" (50 min)
**Era focus: Present ↔ Past.** HELIX arrives topside with paperwork and drones; the dig is now on a clock. To read the chamber wall, Evara needs the script — so Vasudha teaches her the truth her laughed-at thesis was built on: the signs are *labels*, an indexing system for the city's knowledge-house (a real archive of tablets, oral record-keepers, and measured models). Gameplay: a chained deciphering sequence — learn a sign in the past, find it eroded in the present, confirm it in WARDEN's future scans. The player can **call Sakshi** at decipherment stumbles; her mother, who theorized all of this from fragments alone thirty years ago, supplies the hint the field never let her publish — and the moment Evara realizes her mother was *right*, decades too early, is the chapter's quiet emotional peak (Evara can't yet tell her *how* she knows — that reveal is saved for later). Mid-chapter set piece: a trench collapse in the present traps Ishita; Evara must shift to the past, memorize the intact drainage channel layout, shift back, and dig the rescue route (timed; failure/retry) — the layout is exactly the one Sakshi always insisted mattered. Chapter ends decoding the chamber wall's header: **"WHAT THE WATER MUST NOT TAKE."** It is a manifest. The skeleton was a courier — a previous Keeper — who died mid-delivery.

### CHAPTER 3 — "The Engineer's Gambit" (55 min)
**Era focus: Past, heavy puzzle chapter.** The monsoon fails on schedule; Dholarath's east wells go saline. Vasudha proposes her heresy to the council: abandon the lower city *now*, convert the great reservoirs to a survival ration system, and evacuate in stages over two years. Raktim buries the proposal. Gameplay: Evara and Vasudha run a covert demonstration — the player operates the reservoir sluice network (flow-routing puzzles using real Harappan hydraulics) to prove her ration model works, while avoiding council watchmen (stealth; failure = capture, retry from checkpoint). It works. It doesn't matter. Raktim wins the vote by revealing Evara is no trader — a spy, a bad omen. Evara escapes by shifting out. **Low point:** she tries to brute-force history — warning crowds, sabotaging the docks to force evacuation. The Wheel's rule holds: anchored events heal. A furious, grieving Evara confronts Vasudha: *"Then what is this thing FOR?"* Her answer is the game's thesis: **"The river cannot be argued with. But the river only takes what no one carries."**

### CHAPTER 4 — "Two Floods" (55 min)
**Era focus: All three, intercut — the game's structural showpiece.** The manifest becomes a mission: gather Dholarath's essential knowledge — the hydraulic models, the sign index, the seed-grain strains, Vasudha's flood-timing tables — and seal them where each era can hand them to the next (the banyan trick at full scale: past-hides → present-finds → future-verifies). Meanwhile in 2026, an early cyclone bears down on the dig, and HELIX uses the evacuation order to move on the chamber. And in 2087, WARDEN reveals *why* it has been waiting: its founding archive — the thing the Archive drones were built around — is a twenty-first-century recovery of the Dholarath cache. **The future Evara saw only exists because she succeeds.** The chapter climaxes with the two floods intercut: the drowning of Dholarath's lower city in 1900 BCE (escape sequence; carry the child Nira to the causeway; failure/retry) and the cyclone strike on the dig in 2026 (protect the chamber from HELIX under storm cover, with Ishita — who has now seen Evara vanish and return, and chooses to believe her). Vasudha refuses the offer to be "carried" to another century: *"An engineer stays with her water."* She stays. The player does not get to change this. Threaded through the storm: with the phone line failing and the cyclone closing in, Evara finally tells Sakshi the truth — not just *that* the theory is right, but *how* she knows. Her mother, thirty years vindicated in a single impossible phone call, doesn't ask her to prove it or come home. She says the thing an archaeologist mother would say: *"Then record it. All of it. Before the water does."* It reframes the whole mission — and it's the last clear call before the line goes down.

### CHAPTER 5 — "The Keeper's Ledger" (50 min)
**Era focus: Future → Present.** Aftermath in both drowned worlds. In 2087, WARDEN grants Evara "Keeper credential" access to the deep archive — and the twist lands: the ledger of Keepers. The skeleton in the chamber was not Harappan. Carbon-dated 2140 CE. **Keepers come from many centuries; the Wheel has been passed hand to hand across all of them; Vasudha has met three.** Evara is not discovering a phenomenon — she has been *recruited* into a relay older and larger than any single civilization. Gameplay: the archive descent (traversal + the game's hardest three-era puzzle chain, using every verb taught so far) to recover the founding cache intact. Return to 2026: endgame with HELIX — not a gunfight but a heist in reverse: get the cache registered, public, and undeniable before Dhillon's injunction lands (timed multi-era sequence; failure/retry). Marcus Dhillon's last scene is quiet and unsettling: HELIX has scan data from *other* sites. "Yours isn't the only wheel, kid."

### FINALE — "The Signboard" (30 min)
Evara restores the great signboard sequence with the full index and finally reads the north gate's ten-sign inscription the way its makers meant it — not a name, not a prayer. **An address list.** The knowledge-house of Dholarath was one node of a network the Harappans traded with and learned from: sign-groups the index maps to the Nile, to Mesopotamia, to the Yellow River, to sites with no modern name. The Wheel is one of several. The relay is planetary. Closing scene mirrors the opening: Evara at the trench at first light, Ishita beside her, the monsoon holding off, her father's trowel in the dirt. She turns the Wheel one notch past its stop — a fourth "morning" the game never showed — and the screen cuts to black on a sound the player has never heard: a desert wind, and somewhere far off, the Nile.
**Post-credits:** In a Cairo storage vault, a crated bronze disc begins to hum. *KAALCHAKRA will return.*

## 6. Themes (what the game is about)

**You cannot save everything. You can save something.** The refusal to let the player rescue Dholarath is the game's identity — grief and stewardship instead of power fantasy. **Knowledge is the only cargo that survives water.** Every mechanic (indexing, caching, carrying across eras) restates this. **Knowledge is inherited, not owned.** The mother-daughter relay runs through the whole game at three scales: Sakshi hands her theory to Evara, Vasudha hands her city's knowledge to Evara across 4,000 years, and the Keepers hand the Wheel down a chain of centuries — the same act of a woman carrying an older woman's work forward, nested inside itself. **The future is a place, not a fate.** 2087 is not fixed doom; it is what 2026 is currently choosing — the one timeline the player's actions visibly soften by the finale (the future's flood markers recede one band; small, earned, unmissable). And underneath it all: **archaeology as an act of love** — Evara's creed, "we don't take, we record," versus HELIX's "assets," versus WARDEN's cold catalogue, with Vasudha and Sakshi as the answer to all three.

## 7. Failure, Retrial, and the 5-Hour Shape

Failure states are diegetic and fast to retry (checkpoint within 30–60 seconds of every fail point): social-stealth ejections (Ch. 1), timed rescue collapse (Ch. 2), watchman capture (Ch. 3), flood-escape and storm-defense deaths (Ch. 4), heist timer expiry (Ch. 5). Expected first playthrough with normal failure: **4.5–5.5 hours.** Optional content that does not extend the critical path: 12 collectible "ledger entries" (past Keepers' notes across all three eras), 5 optional Vasudha engineering conversations (deepen the hydraulics lore), and 3 WARDEN catalogue queries that quietly foreshadow Volume Two's civilizations.

## 8. The Franchise Map (Volumes Two and Beyond)

The finale's address list is the series bible in miniature. Each volume: same Keeper (or a new Keeper meeting Evara — the relay structure allows both), one civilization, three mornings, one thing that cannot be saved and one thing that can.

- **Vol. 2 — Egypt:** the Nile node; a wheel in a Cairo vault; the past era is the Old Kingdom's drought collapse (First Intermediate Period).
- **Vol. 3 — Mesopotamia:** Ur and the salt death of the fields; the oldest node; where the relay *began* — or seems to.
- **Vol. 4 — the Americas / Maya:** the node the index maps with signs even Vasudha's people couldn't read; the first hint the network is older than every civilization on the list.
- **Series endgame question,** seeded from Vol. 1's skeleton (a Keeper from 2140): the relay runs *forward* too. Someone in the deep future is the recipient of everything the Keepers have ever carried. Volume One never asks who. Later volumes do.
